A well-worn codex that appeared on the doorstep of the Dragonslayers' villa. The sender is unknown, but can be guessed to be an agent of the Order of the Eclipse. 

The book details the history of the Order, as well as anecdotes and recorded cases where *contracts of Righteous Retribution* were either granted or denied. The Order's strictures and other types of contracts that are more rarely granted are also detailed.

"Calefa's Order exists to prevent the degeneration of the Empire into a snarl of squabbling noble houses, as well as to serve as the Emperor's covert hand against enemies of state."

"When a contractor proves that the intended target is responsible for the wrongful death of a family member or close friend; and that death has not been compensated by court, tribunal, prior assassination, or previous personal action; then a *contract of Righteous Retribution* may be issued. A death by *Righteous Retribution* is never wrongful; the Order will never need to revenge against its own deeds."

"Other contracts that may be granted are *contracts of Merciful Release*, to free a person being kept alive against their will solely for the benefit of others; *contracts of Honorable Passage",* where a contractor may request redemption for shameful deeds; and several esoteric and seldom used options."

"The Tarkaun may also, in his wise judgement guided by the will of Ammonar, issue orders of assassination against his enemies. As the Emperor is the Empire embodied, actions against the Empire are actions against the sacrosanct person of the Emperor himself - and are thus punishable by death."

"Famous and instructive examples of how contracts are granted follow:"

- A patrician of House Varias murdered a rival of House Lazar in a fit of jealous envy. Though sentenced to death at trial, the patrician was pardoned by his sister, a Keeper of the Hearth, and suffered no penalty. The son of the deceased Lazar **was granted righteous retribution**, and the Order assassinated the murderous Varias.

- While an armiger was on campaign with the legions, his daughter was raped and murdered by his neighbor. No one stood to prosecute, and by the time the armiger returned from war, it was too late to press his claim in court. The armiger **was granted righteous retribution**, and the Order assassinated the murderous neighbor.

- A prominent templar was trialed at court for the murder of his wife and was found guilty. The sentence laid down was confiscation of property and indenture for five years. The wife’s father **was not granted retribution**, as the templar had already been punished in court.

- A knight of House Valerian learned that a patrician of House Drakomir had killed his brother in a drunken duel. Outraged, he assaulted the Drakomir, cutting off his hand. Later, the Valerian sought righteous retribution and attempted to contract for the Drakomir’s assassination. He **was not granted retribution,** as he had already taken personal action against the Drakomir.

"A full accounting of contracts granted, to whom and for what purpose, is recorded in the Codex of Calefa. Members of the Order may consult the Codex, but it is forbidden to all others save his Majesty, the most divine Tarkaun."
